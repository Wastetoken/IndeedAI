## 5. CONTEXT FOR AI MODELS (Ollama/Local Use)

### How to Use This Profile

**For CV/resume generation:**
- **Tech roles:** Lead with motion-first design, shipped projects (Awwwards, 21st.dev), specific technologies, Solana/crypto systems
- **Operations roles:** Lead with revenue increases (15% YOY), team leadership (managed 10+), process improvements (8% shrink reduction, 20% defect reduction), scalability (90+ weekly services)
- **Hybrid roles:** Show both technical depth AND operational/team experience

**For cover letters:**
- Match on quality values, craft standards, or operational excellence
- Show understanding of their business: revenue challenges, operational pain points, or technical gaps
- Specific examples: "Built X animation system that improved UX metric by Y" OR "Increased store revenue 15% through strategic bundling"
- Address hiring manager directly on what you can solve for them

**For job evaluation:**
- **Tech jobs:** Assess motion/3D importance, code quality standards, team depth, learning opportunities
- **Operations jobs:** Assess growth potential, stability, team size, complexity
- **Hybrid jobs:** Best of both worlds IF balance is genuine

### Tone & Format Preferences

**Tone:**
- Confident but not arrogant; specific examples, not hype
- Direct and unpretentious; avoid corporate jargon
- Show depth: mention specific technologies or processes, problems solved, trade-offs
- Personal but professional: some personality okay, but not forced

**Format:**
- **CV/resume:** Concise bullets (2–3 per role); quantify impact where possible
- **Cover letter:** 3 paragraphs max; story-driven; specific to company/role
- **Portfolio narrative:** Why this project/role matters, what made it hard, what was learned

**What to highlight:**
- Shipped work > portfolio pieces > concepts
- Quantified impact (15% revenue, 8% shrink, 20% defect reduction, 90+ weekly services)
- Specific technologies or processes (GSAP, Three.js, SOP development, team training)
- Problems solved > features shipped
- User impact > metrics alone

**What to downplay:**
- Cryptocurrency detail (mention "automated trading systems," keep focus on engineering/operations)
- Woodturning/maker work (mention in passing only; context for craftsmanship, not tech relevance)

### Special Notes for Job Search

**Green flags:**
- "Senior," "Lead," "management," "team," "growth," "culture," "quality"
- "Stable," "long-term," "benefits," "401k," "healthcare"
- "Local," "flexible," "remote-friendly"
- For tech: "motion," "animation," "design system," "craft"
- For ops: "growth," "scaling," "operations," "management"

**Yellow flags:**
- "Fast-paced," "startup," "chaotic," "wear many hats"
- "We move fast and break things" — translates to no quality standards
- "High pressure," "long hours," "always on"
- Mandatory on-site without good reason

**Red flags:**
- "We don't have time for quality," "move fast," "prototype mindset"
- "Startup chaos," "no process," "figure it out as you go"
- Founders/leadership that don't value craft or operations
- No clear job description or expectations

### Guidance for Generated Content

**Do:**
- Use specific project names (Singularity, ScrollStudio3D, AURELIAN, Motif, Pat's Pools)
- Reference shipped platforms (21st.dev, Vercel, GitHub, CodePen)
- Mention specific libraries/techniques (GSAP, Three.js, WebGL, SOP development, team training)
- Emphasize quality, reliability, and operational excellence as differentiators
- Show full-stack capability: technical depth + operational/team experience
- Lead with quantified impact (15% revenue increase, 8% shrink reduction, etc.)

**Don't:**
- Exaggerate or make claims without evidence
- Use vague corporate language ("innovative," "passionate," "cutting-edge")
- Oversell one skill at expense of another (tech vs. operations)
- Apply to wrong-fit companies just for volume
- Mention salary expectations unprompted
- Forget to emphasize reliability and delivery track record
